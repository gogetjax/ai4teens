# Phase 4 — Present

Close the AI. Open your present-back worksheet. You are giving a short illustrated
talk on your object, plus a short report on the tools you used.

## The diagram walkthrough (your object)
1. **Name the object and the view** you chose, and why that view shows it best.
2. **Walk the parts.** Point at each labeled part and say, in your own words, what
   it is and what it does. From memory, not by reading the label back.
3. **Tell the truth about one part** that is easy to get wrong, or that the model
   kept getting wrong.

## The model report (your tools)
4. **Which model won, and on what.** Accuracy, labels, legibility, looks. Be
   specific: "Model B drew the layers in the right order but spelled two labels
   wrong, so I picked Model A and fixed the layout."
5. **Show one error you caught** and how you knew it was wrong. This is the most
   important thirty seconds of the talk. Catching the lie is the skill.
6. **Say what you would change** about your prompt if you ran it again.

Then present to the group. Expect questions. The group should point at a part and
ask what it does, and ask which label you trust least and why. If you can answer
without your notes, and you can name an error the model made, you have actually
learned both the object and the tool.

A strong present-back is not the prettiest picture. It is the student who can say
"this part here is X, it does Y, and by the way the first model put it in the
wrong place, which is how I knew not to trust it."
