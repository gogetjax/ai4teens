# Student Object Ideas

Pick **one** object you actually want to understand better. The best choice is
something you use or see often but could not really explain if someone asked. You
do not have to pick from this list; it is here to get you started.

A good object has clear, nameable parts and a reason worth explaining. If you can
already list every part from memory, pick something harder.

## Biological systems
- The human heart (chambers, valves, the path blood takes)
- The human ear (outer, middle, inner, how sound becomes a signal)
- A neuron (the parts of a nerve cell and how a signal travels)
- A tooth (enamel, dentin, pulp, root)

## Food and plants
- A cacao pod (pod, pulp, beans, and how it becomes chocolate)
- A flower (the parts and how pollination works)
- A seed (coat, embryo, food store, and how it sprouts)
- A leaf (layers and how photosynthesis happens inside)

## Made things
- A cellphone (screen, battery, processor, camera, antenna)
- A printer (how ink or toner actually gets onto paper)
- A battery (the parts and how chemistry becomes electricity)
- A pencil or a ballpoint pen (simpler than you think, and a good first try)
- A lock and key, a bicycle gear system, a pair of headphones

## How to choose well
- One object, not a scene. "A car engine" works; "a city" does not.
- Pick something with five to ten parts you can name and define.
- Pick something with real specs you can look up: a size, a weight, a rating. Your
  infographic has a specifications panel, and checking those numbers against the
  model is half the lesson.
- Pick something where getting a part wrong would actually matter. That is what
  makes catching the model's errors worth doing.

Once you have chosen, go to `phase-1-know-your-object.md` and start your spec.
