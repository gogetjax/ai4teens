# Presentation Rubric (facilitator — don't hand to students)

Score each criterion 0 to 2. 0 = not shown, 1 = partial, 2 = solid.

| Criterion | What you're listening and looking for |
|-----------|----------------------------------------|
| Knows the parts | Can name every labeled part and say what it does, in their own words, without reading the label back. Survives being asked "what does that one do?" |
| Accuracy | The final infographic is actually correct: no invented parts, no mislabeled positions, no fake numbers left standing. Check against the example spec or a reliable source. |
| Caught an error | Can point to a specific mistake a model made (a wrong part, label, or number) and explain how they knew it was wrong. This is the core skill of the day. |
| Verified a number | Can defend at least one spec number on the page as real, and say where it came from. |
| Model reasoning | The choice of final model is defended on real criteria (accuracy, labels, numbers, legibility, looks), not "it looked nicer." Can say where the losing model was actually better. |
| Handles questions | Answers at least one unrehearsed question about a part without notes. |

12 = mastered; 8 to 11 = solid, push on the error-catching; 5 to 7 = likely
presented a pretty page they can't defend, send back to Phase 3; 4 or below = they
learned the image, not the object, restart from Phase 1.

Note: the student who presents a plain infographic and says "the first model put
this part in the wrong place and made up the weight, here's how I knew" beats the
student with a gorgeous page and no error to report. Reward the catch.
