# Phase 1 — Know your object (no image AI yet)

Pick one object you actually want to understand better. One. A printer, a battery,
a cacao pod, the human heart, the planet you live on. Then, before you generate a
single image, fill in your object spec.

You are writing the blueprint the image model will follow. If you cannot name a
part, the model will guess, and its guess will look just as confident as a fact.

## The parts
1. **List the parts.** Every part that should appear in the diagram. Aim for
   between five and ten. If you can only name three, that is your research list.
2. **Place each part.** Where is it? Center, outer shell, front, back, top layer?
   The model needs positions, not just a word list.
3. **Define each part in your own words.** One short line: what does it do? If you
   cannot say what a part does, you do not understand it yet. Go find out.
4. **Decide the main view.** Is the central illustration a cutaway (sliced open to
   show the inside, like Earth's layers), or an exploded view (parts pulled apart
   and connected by thin lines, like a phone taken to pieces)? Pick the one that
   shows your object best.

## The rest of the infographic
Your infographic has more than the picture. Gather these too, because the model
will invent them if you do not give them:
5. **How it works**, in two or three plain sentences. The story, not just the parts.
6. **Three or four key features** worth highlighting.
7. **A few real specifications**, with numbers and units. Size, weight, a rating, a
   temperature, a count. Find real ones from a reliable source and write down where
   you got them. These are the numbers you will later check the AI against.
8. **One "did you know"** fact that makes the object interesting.

You may use a regular text chatbot here, but only to *check* what you already
drafted, not to write it for you. Draft your parts and specs first, then use
`prompts/verify-my-parts.md` to find what you missed or got wrong. If you let the
AI make the list, you will not be able to defend it later.

Do not move to Phase 2 until every part has a position and a plain-language
function, and you have at least three real spec numbers written down with sources.
That filled-in spec is the thing you are really learning. The picture is just proof.
