# Three Worked Examples — Distilling Dense Text

Show these before students start, to model the moves. Each takes a dense sentence
and distills it a different way.

## Example 1 — Define the nouns first
**Dense:** "The processor throttles clock frequency under sustained thermal load to
preserve die integrity."
**Move:** Underline the nouns and define only those. *Throttle* = deliberately slow
down. *Clock frequency* = how many operations per second. *Thermal load* = built-up
heat. *Die* = the actual silicon chip.
**Distilled:** "When the chip gets too hot for too long, it slows itself down so it
doesn't damage itself."
**Lesson:** Most "hard" sentences are easy sentences wearing three unfamiliar nouns.
Define the nouns and the sentence often collapses into something obvious.

## Example 2 — Ladder down to something you already know
**Dense:** "Inflation erodes the purchasing power of savings."
**Move:** Keep asking "but what is THAT?" until you hit familiar ground. Purchasing
power = how much stuff your money buys. Inflation = prices generally rising over
time. Erodes = wears down slowly. Now climb back up.
**Distilled:** "When prices keep rising, the money you saved buys less than it used
to — so just sitting on cash quietly loses value."
**Lesson:** You don't have to understand a hard sentence all at once. Drop down one
word at a time until you reach something you already know, then build back up.

## Example 3 — Trade it for an analogy, then check the seams
**Dense:** "A vaccine trains the immune system using a harmless facsimile of the
pathogen."
**Move:** Swap the concept for something familiar — *then deliberately find where the
analogy lies.*
**Analogy:** "It's like showing your body a wanted poster of a criminal so it can
recognize them later."
**Where it breaks:** A wanted poster is just a picture; a vaccine actually makes your
body *practice* the response and keep a memory of it. The poster gets "recognize
later" right but misses the "build and store a defense" part.
**Lesson:** Analogies are the fastest way to *grasp* something and the fastest way to
be *subtly wrong*. Always say the analogy AND where it fails — that's the difference
between sounding like you understand and understanding.
