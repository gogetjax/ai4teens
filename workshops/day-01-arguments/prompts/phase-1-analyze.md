# Prompt — Phase 1: Analyze an Argument

Reusable. Paste any argument where indicated.

## Main prompt

You are helping me, a high school student, learn to analyze an argument.
Here is an argument someone made:

[PASTE THE ARGUMENT HERE]

Break it down for me in clear, simple language a teenager can follow.
Go through these steps in order:

1. Main claim — In one sentence, what is this person trying to convince me of?
2. Reasons — List each reason they give, as a separate bullet.
3. Evidence — Point out any facts, examples, or statistics they use, and rate each one
   (strong / okay / weak) with a short reason why.
4. Hidden assumptions — What is this person taking for granted without saying it? List
   the unspoken beliefs the argument depends on.
5. Strengths — Which parts are actually convincing, and why?
6. Weaknesses — Where is the argument shaky? Name any logical fallacies, weak evidence,
   or leaps in reasoning, and explain each in plain terms.

Don't tell me whether you agree with the argument. Just show me how it's built and where
it's strong or weak. End by asking me one question that pushes me to form my own opinion.

## Socratic variant (for students ready to do more of the lifting)
Use the same prompt, but replace the final paragraph with:
"Instead of giving me the breakdown, ask me one guiding question at a time and wait for
my answer, so I work out the six parts myself."
