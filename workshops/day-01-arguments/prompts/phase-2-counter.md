# Prompts — Phase 2: Counterargument

Reusable. Paste the original argument (and, for Prompt B, the counterargument) where
indicated.

## Prompt A — Build a counterargument that targets weaknesses

You are helping me, a high school student, learn to build a strong counterargument.
Here is the original argument:

[PASTE THE ORIGINAL ARGUMENT HERE]

Here are the weaknesses I found in it:

[PASTE YOUR LIST OF WEAKNESSES — or write "you find them" if I left this blank]

Write a counterargument to the original. As you do:
- For each point you make, name which specific weakness it targets.
- Identify the single STRONGEST point in the original, and address it honestly instead of
  ignoring it or twisting it into something easier to beat.
- Use reasoning or realistic evidence, not just confident assertions.
- Don't insult the person or attack their motives — beat the argument, not the arguer.
- And actually write the argument itself, not tell me about the argument to write. 

After the counterargument, add a short note: which part of your counter is the most solid,
and which part is the weakest or easiest to push back on? End by asking me one question
that makes me decide how I'd strengthen it.

## Prompt B — Evaluate a counterargument

You are helping me, a high school student, judge how good a counterargument is.

Original argument:
[PASTE THE ORIGINAL ARGUMENT HERE]

Counterargument to evaluate:
[PASTE THE COUNTERARGUMENT HERE]

Evaluate the counterargument honestly. Tell me:
1. Which real weaknesses of the original does it actually hit?
2. Does it deal with the original's strongest point, or dodge it?
3. Does it strawman the original (attack a weaker version than what was said)?
4. Does the counterargument commit any fallacies or weak moves of its own?
5. Overall: how effective is it, and what one change would make it stronger?

Don't just praise it — point out where it's weak. End by asking me one question that pushes
me to improve it myself.
