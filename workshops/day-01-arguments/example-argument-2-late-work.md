# Example Argument 2 — Late Work

Riley is a fictional student. Students run the Phase 1 and Phase 2 prompts on this argument
the same way they did with the Jordan example.

## The argument (posted in a class group chat)

> Teachers need to stop taking points off for late work. A grade is supposed to measure what
> you actually learned, not what day you happened to hit submit — a perfect essay is a perfect
> essay whether it's Tuesday or Thursday. On top of that, not everyone has the same life: some
> of us work jobs, take care of siblings, or are just struggling, and deadlines punish those
> kids hardest for things that have nothing to do with the subject. My brother had a teacher
> last year who took late work with no penalty, and that class had some of the highest grades
> in the school, so clearly it works. The only reason teachers cling to deadlines is that they
> like having control — and real life doesn't even work that way.

---

## Facilitator answer key (don't hand this to students)

- "A grade should measure learning, not timing" — the STRONGEST point; a defensible philosophy
  of assessment. A good counterargument must engage it, not dodge it.
- "Deadlines punish kids with harder lives" — a real equity concern, but overgeneralized: it
  slides from "some students need flexibility" to "no one should have deadlines."
- Brother's class — anecdotal, a sample of one; correlation isn't causation.
- "Teachers like control" — attacks motives (ad hominem).
- "Real life doesn't work that way" — actually reversed; real life is full of hard deadlines,
  so the point undercuts itself.
