# Example Argument 3 — Using AI to Write Essays

Sam is a fictional student. This argument is a useful mirror: the natural counterargument is
the course's own stance — think *with* AI, don't outsource your thinking to it.

## The argument (posted in a class group chat)

> Honestly, the school should just let us use AI like ChatGPT to write our essays. When
> calculators came out, teachers freaked and banned them too — now everyone uses them and
> nobody cares, and AI is the exact same thing. Companies use AI for writing constantly, so
> banning it in school just guarantees we show up to our first jobs totally unprepared. And
> let's be real: teachers can't even reliably tell what's AI-written, so the rule does nothing
> but turn careful students into "cheaters" over something that can't be enforced. Anyone who's
> still against AI essays just doesn't really understand the technology.

---

## Facilitator answer key (don't hand this to students)

- "Employers use AI, so banning it leaves us unprepared" — the STRONGEST point; AI literacy
  genuinely matters. A good counterargument concedes it, then separates *using* AI from having
  AI *do the thinking*.
- Calculator analogy — weak/false analogy: a calculator automates a step you already understand,
  while AI writing the whole essay replaces the skill being taught. (Kernel of truth: tools do
  get normalized — worth acknowledging.)
- "Teachers can't detect it, so the rule is pointless" — same shape as Jordan's "everyone does
  it anyway"; hard to enforce is not the same as not worth having.
- "Anyone against it doesn't understand technology" — ad hominem.
